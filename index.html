How a pen is cut

Three cuts, in this order, no going back

  1. The shoulder

    The reed is opened at a long shallow angle so the ink has somewhere to sit. Cut it short and the pen runs dry mid-letter.

  2. The slit

    One press splits the nib down the centre. It has to stop exactly where the shoulder ends, or the two halves will spread under pressure.

  3. The qatt

    The tip is trimmed against the qat'ah at the angle your script needs — flat for thuluth, oblique for nastaliq. This is the cut you cannot take back.

Questions

Can I order before the shop opens?

No. Lot One is cut in a single run and every piece goes to the list in the order names came in. Adding your name is the whole queue.

Do you cut for left hands?

Yes. Say so when you order and the qatt is reversed. There is no extra charge and no extra wait.

How long does a reed pen last?

A season of daily practice, then it wants re-cutting. We send a qat'ah with every pen and instructions for the second cut.

Do you ship outside the country?

Not for Lot One. Ink is a problem at customs and we would rather solve it properly than lose your parcel.

The shop opens once. Be on the list.
